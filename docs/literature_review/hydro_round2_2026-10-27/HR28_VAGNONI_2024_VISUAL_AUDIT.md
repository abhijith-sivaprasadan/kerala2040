## Executive conclusion

This is a useful *European hydropower flexibility and modernization* source drawn from the COST Action Pen@Hydropower network, cited IEA/JRC flexibility forecasts, two distinct expert survey streams and illustrative technology work. **It does not present an independently reproduced European balancing model, a probability sample of EU hydropower plants, a solved water–electricity optimization, measured digitalization gains or a Kerala case study.**

### Most consequential source distinctions

- **PDF pp2–3, Figure 3:** 2030 daily, weekly and monthly flexibility amounts are 288, 258 and 173 **TWh per year**, respectively, for different balancing timescales. They are not TWh per day or installed power. Claims of 133%, 166% and 300% growth rely on an external scenario model. Rounded implied 2021 baselines are **123.61, 96.99 and 43.25 TWh/year**; these are arithmetic, not independently recovered source observations. Categories may overlap and must not simply be summed.
- **PDF pp1,3–4:** More than **30%** of 2030 European flexibility is a **cited scenario projection**, not newly computed by this paper or validated against plant operations.
- **PDF pp4–5, Figure 4:** flexibility survey caption specifies **15 European countries**, while several responses appear in ~3.8-percentage-point increments, consistent with about 26 questionnaire records in those panels. The exact per-question response count, multiple participation per country and weighting are not reported.
- **PDF pp5–9, Figures 8–14:** a **different** technology/digitalization questionnaire is associated with WG2's 97 members from 27 countries; the pie responses show 10-percentage-point increments but raw answer counts and statistical representativeness are missing.
- **PDF pp6–7, Figures 5–7:** the turbine photograph, oil-system correlation heatmap and geometry optimization workflow illustrate methods; none supplies an independently demonstrated plant-efficiency uplift, fault-detection accuracy or fatigue improvement.
- **PDF p7:** text incorrectly points back to Figure 8 (the questionnaire) as the first unmanned-operation results; Figure 9 actually shows those results.
- **PDF pp7–9:** 60% of responding experts say a majority of plants in *their countries* have unmanned operation / modern SCADA; this is not a direct claim about 60% of *all plants in Europe*. Likewise 90% reporting a national drinking-water hydro installation is not a plant-level penetration rate.
- **PDF pp5,9–10:** start/stop fatigue, environmental flows, fish passage, hydropeaking, sediment and digital cybersecurity are important but not represented in quantitative optimization or verified refurbishment economics.

## Implications for Kerala2040

The study identifies potential **hydropower asset readiness and operational constraint inputs**, not a substitute for Kerala data. Measure unit-specific head/MW, water storage, gate rule limits, ramping and fatigue, environmental release requirements, digital SCADA readiness, actual prices and transmission deliverability.

**Cross-review:** HR25 provides India's PSP execution risk; HR38 supplies an implemented China hydro-PSP dispatch comparator with unresolved published equations; HR37 provides conceptual water–power interdependency and resilience. HR28 complements these as a European modernization and short/long-term flexibility review. Generic hydropower-PSP integration, CFD design or sensor digitalization is **not** a defensible standalone novelty claim.
