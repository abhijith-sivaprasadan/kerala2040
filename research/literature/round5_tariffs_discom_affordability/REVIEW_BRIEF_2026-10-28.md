# Kerala2040 — Round 5 scoping brief (28 October 2026)

## Topic: Can a low-carbon Kerala electricity transition be financially and socially sustainable?

**Status:** research-gap screening with publisher/official web provenance, **not** a completed full-text study, empirical financial analysis, independent regulator interpretation or finished peer-reviewed manuscript. The earlier one-manuscript Paper A/A0 charter is unchanged.

The first three previous reviews tested weather-sensitive load, hydro/water flexibility and renewable electrical deliverability. Round 4 screened physical climate-grid outage resilience. **Round 5** moves to the financial and social layer: tariff classes, utility cost recovery, cross-subsidy and renewable power-purchase effects, household energy burden, political/institutional decisions and affordability.

## Why this matters for Kerala and what is already known

KSEBL filed tariff-revision petitions and ARR/expected-revenue material for 2022–27. **These are filings rather than automatically approved costs.** Separate KSERC orders, the regulator's later ToD and fixed-charge clarifications, and PFC's **February 2026** *Performance of Power Utilities 2024–25* create a plausible three-part evidence chain. The available regulator and central-utility reporting are source listings, **not a reconciled longitudinal analytic dataset in this round**.

National academic prior art is substantial: tariff cross-subsidization has already been studied with an **18-state 10-year panel** by [Mehta and Sarangi (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2022.113157); [Das and Srikanth (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2020.111882) analyse DISCOM procurement and viability; [Rodrigues, Iychettira and Lane (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tej.2026.107533) investigate RE adoption's conditional effects on two state utilities; [Ghodeswar et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tej.2026.107530) study 2007–20 utility balance sheet factors. Therefore “India has DISCOM problems,” “cross-subsidies exist,” or “renewables affect DISCOM revenue” cannot constitute novelty by themselves.

On the consumer side, [Sadath and Acharya (2025)](https://doi.org/10.32479/ijeep.18435) use different energy-poverty measures and show that results vary between access and affordability definitions. [Manasi and Mukhopadhyay (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esd.2024.101383), [Haider et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jup.2024.101735), [Sedai et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2020.104975) and [Jose and Patra (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1759-3441.70006) reinforce the critical distinction between **connection, reliability, electricity consumption, household expenditure and ability to pay**. None establishes a measured present Kerala household electricity affordability curve without matched Kerala source observations.

## Five falsifiable paper-admission questions

1. **Financial boundaries**: Are publicly reported KSEB deficits, tariff revenue gaps, AT&C losses and cash collections materially different measurements, and can an original-source crosswalk document the gap without smoothing disagreements?
2. **Approved vs proposed tariffs**: Do later KSERC orders and corrections materially change sample electricity bills or consumer-class cross-subsidy estimates compared with popular simplified tariff tables? Requires final order and effective dates, not drafts.
3. **Cross-subsidy tension**: If high-paying industrial/commercial loads migrate to self-generation/open access, can a documented source/model identify whether net DISCOM loss increases? Or is this a scenario requiring unavailable customer-class volumes and contract terms?
4. **Affordability**: Can electricity bills be compared with a defensible **survey household income/expenditure denominator** using matched year and household class, or is the most the paper can provide illustrative bills and source limitations?
5. **Public-sector fiscal transparency**: Can claims, sanctions, accrual and cash-paid subsidies be separated in original records and reconciled with reported utility receivables? Without original data, report the missingness as the finding.

2. **KSEBL tariff petition** for 2023–27 and original KSERC approvals; approved versus requested, revision dates, valid tariff-category mapping.

## Candidate Kerala novelty — conditional

A reusable **source-audited tariff and DISCOM-finance boundary crosswalk** might be defensible if it reconciles petitions, orders, true-ups, cost reports and payment status at consistent FY and consumer-category levels. A second concept—**billing affordability and distributional impact of policy changes**—needs original lawful microdata/income denominators and may therefore be ruled out.

A paper that only says Kerala tariffs can rise while solar grows or that power distribution is financially stressed is not sufficient novelty. If original Kerala utility and household data cannot support a real independent comparison, produce a transparent evidence catalogue rather than a contrived fourth/fifth article.
