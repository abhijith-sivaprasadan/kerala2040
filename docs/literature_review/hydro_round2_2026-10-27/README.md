# Kerala2040 — Round 2 hydropower and reservoir flexibility

**Date:** 27 October 2026. **Status:** 40 unique publications screened from identifiable publisher/institutional bibliographic metadata and abstracts. **Not 40 full-text visually inspected papers.** The original 20 individually reviewed documents remain a separate corpus.

## Research question

How much reliable hydropower flexibility can Kerala provide across monsoon and dry seasons when reservoir inflows, head, storage, flood-release rules, forecast uncertainty, non-power obligations, peak loads and transmission capability are evaluated together?

## Preliminary synthesis

1. **Flood operations are studied already.** The 2018 Kerala studies examine reservoir/rainfall attribution, guide curves, historical simulations and satellite-based reconstruction. This review does not claim novelty for reservoir modelling itself.
2. **Flood causality and avoided flood impacts are different estimands.** Heavy rainfall can dominate an observed event while an alternative feasible reservoir operation still attenuates its peak. Compare basin, location, date, flow and counterfactual.
3. **Power-system flexibility is not installed hydro MW.** Count deliverable MW, duration, storage state, water head and obligations, ramp rates, reserves and intertemporal effects.
4. **Forecast information has a cutoff.** Using retrospective ERA5 or post-event storage is not equivalent to an operational forecast available to the dispatcher.
5. **Existing Kerala transmission evidence is a planning input, not a runnable PSS/E case.** Coupled adequacy claims require observed time series, network parameters and independently checked contingency tests.
6. **Candidate novelty, not yet confirmed:** a coherent Kerala case study with verified hydro water balances and physically credible evening-peak electricity dispatch under environmental, flood, weather and import constraints.

**Screening boundary:** bibliographic and abstract-level evidence only; figure-by-figure, equation-by-equation and simulation reproduction remain outstanding. Search was targeted English-language public discovery (no original hit counts, no systematic PRISMA claim). Working/technical papers are clearly separate evidence classes. Third-party PDFs should not be redistributed without rights review.
