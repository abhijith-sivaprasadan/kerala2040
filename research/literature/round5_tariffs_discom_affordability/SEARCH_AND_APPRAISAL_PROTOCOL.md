# Round 5 — search, classification, full-paper appraisal and evidence admission protocol

**Date of screening:** 28 October 2026 (Stockholm local). **Classification:** targeted critical-narrative research screening and literature-gaps register, **not** PRISMA systematic review, complete full-text critical analysis, preregistered causal analysis, or peer-reviewed publication.

## Topic and strictly separate scope

**Electricity tariffs, Indian state DISCOM financial sustainability, distributional affordability, cross-subsidies, financing and consumer equity.**

The four existing rounds focus on (R1) weather-linked electricity demand and historical data, (R2) reservoir and hydro flexibility, (R3) renewable power grid deliverability and feeder hosting capacity, (R4) extreme-weather physical outages and recovery. R5 is a **financial, regulatory and social acceptance** question: *who pays, who receives subsidies or energy services, what is the actual approved cost and revenue, and can the transition be financed without unmeasured regressivity?*

R5 does not model transmission contingencies, reservoir dispatch, outage restoration or new Kerala demand time-series as substitutes for actual utility and consumer accounting.

### Inclusion criteria

- India/Kerala scholarly journal research in tariff regulation, distribution-utility revenues and cashflow, ACS/ARR, losses and subsidies, electricity affordability, open access, consumer welfare or governance; plus a very limited comparator on dynamic tariff design.
- State or original official government sources: KSERC tariff orders/clarifications/regulations, KSEBL petitions, PFC financial records, FOR reporting, MoP annual reporting, national energy subsidy overview.
- Published and accessible DOI metadata by or before **28 Oct 2026**; older rigorous studies retained for methods and identifying political confounders.
- Sources receive exact article type, access grade and individual measurement/counterfactual questions; policy statements are not empirical data.

### Exclusion criteria

- Round 3 physics: hosting MW, curtailment and network constraints without direct **tariff, customer or DISCOM-finance** endpoints.
- Round 1 statistical weather/demand model comparisons without regulatory/billing outcomes.
- Round 2 hydro physical storage/reservoir dispatch.
- Round 4 disaster outages/restoration without clear costs, utility accounts or socioeconomic affordability as the primary outcome.
- Macroeconomic green-growth extrapolation with weak link to tariff, households or DISCOMs, marketing-only claims, undisclosed numerical sources, not-yet-published future-dated records.
- Legal **petitions** mislabelled final KSERC orders; planning suggestions mislabelled enacted government subsidy; year-specific tariffs silently applied to later years.
- A single study's national elasticity or subsidy coefficient silently transplanted to Kerala.

## Classification and accounting ontology

**Financial and tariff records are incompatible unless their units, boundaries, financial year, subsidy treatment and vintage are explicitly reconciled.**

| Concept | Required definition / disambiguation |
| --- | --- |
| KSERC tariff schedule | Approved effective date, consumer class, fixed vs variable charges, slab, demand charges, duty, surcharge, ToD, prosumer status |
| KSEB tariff **petition** | Proposal, claims, supporting cost estimates, not approved final amounts |
| ARR / ERC | Allowed or claimed annual revenue requirement and expected revenue; normalize for subsidy, true-up, regulatory amortisation, fuel adjustment and accounting vintage |
| ACS–ARR gap | Average Cost of Supply versus Average Revenue Realized; define with and without subsidy/REG before comparing different PFC years |
| AT&C losses | Billing × collection and energy accounting; separate technical losses, theft, unbilled sales and collections |
| Cash deficit / receivables | Financial statement balance/cash basis; **not** equivalent to tariff gap or measured energy deficit |
| Power purchase | Fixed capacity vs energy charges, contracted vs actually scheduled versus metered generation and procurement, renewables obligation |
| Subsidies | Explicit government budget support, booked compensation, implicit cross-subsidy, energy-consumer relief, and direct benefit transfer: **distinct ledgers** |
| Household affordability | Invoice/household burden, income/expenditure denominator and survey period, access, appliances, ability to pay; low kWh consumed can signal deprivation |
| Revenue and customer churn | Customer category contribution, captive/open-access migration, rooftop self-consumption and export; do not assume generic lost sales equal lost profit |
| Fiscal impact | State budget subsidy/grant and utility profit are not identical; match reporting unit and actual payment date |

## Research questions — appraisal, not a new paper commitment

- **RQ5-A:** Is the Kerala-specific actual revenue/cost/cross-subsidy trend inferable by matching original PFC financial-year files, KSEB filed accounts, and KSERC approved and trued-up orders? Where does source disagreement remain?
- **RQ5-B:** Which customer categories subsidize others in original **effective** Kerala tariff schedules, and how are fixed charges, duty, ToD tariffs, prosumers and cross-subsidy surcharges treated?
- **RQ5-C:** Is a household affordability judgement possible with available consumption and **income/expenditure household data**, or does public data reveal tariffs only, not hardship?
- **RQ5-D:** Do published DISCOM financial effects of renewable procurement and cross-subsidy migration identify causal effects or conditional scenarios, and can any coefficients validly transfer to Kerala?
- **RQ5-E:** Can a transparent, reusable **tariff-versus-accounting source-admission protocol** constitute a publishable methods contribution; or is the appropriate output an evidence atlas rather than another paper?

## Full-paper appraisal card (do not mark complete on bibliographic metadata alone)

Record: exact verified DOI and publisher title, author list, article type/year/online date, publisher URL, downloaded PDF origin, lawful source-byte SHA, license; economic units and base currency/year; agency and source-year vintage; observed versus modelled outcomes; sample, geography and subgroups; actual counterfactual identification and sensitivity; expenditure and revenue definitions; whether financial ratios include subsidies; cross-section vs panel vs quasi-experimental limitations; figures/appendix page references; negative or contradictory findings; direct Kerala transferability; source rights.

Primary-source card: official URL, **document issue/effective dates**, case/order number, revised/superseded/court status, petition vs approved vs trued-up, item source page, category/units and sanctioned terms. Independent secondary financial reporting must be kept distinct.

## Admissibility and stopping rules

1. No new causal effect on Kerala household welfare solely from a tariff schedule, a national model or survey in other states.
2. No claim that financial stress proves shortage/load shedding or that annual energy under-delivery represents customer welfare.
3. No tariff burden statistic without actual bill formula, exemption and a suitable household denominator; illustrative example must be labelled.
4. For any economic comparison, record financial-year window and rupee crore / INR per kWh as appropriate, accounting formula and whether subsidy is cash or accrued.
5. Require an executed or final gazetted order before saying a tariff legally applies; historical orders and amendment dates not silently collapsed.
6. Preserve no-APC and one-manuscript 400h charter for 2026–27. Literature Round 5 remains separate queued background research, **not** authority to consume new project work packages.
7. Source data rights and copyright: no public bulk original private customer bills, KSEB accounts or purchased PDFs unless owner rights and approval are checked.
8. No paper marked fully reviewed until complete text, data tables, limitations, figures and supplement have been inspected with page references.
